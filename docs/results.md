# Results: ours against the four baselines

Measured 2026-11-24 on the whole corpus, 47 916 trajectories in nine datasets, with
`greedy-stabbing-shared` (Algorithm 2), after both arithmetic fixes of that day (`geometry::closest`,
and the precision floor of `GreedyStabber::run`). Every number here is regenerated by steps
7–12 of [running.md](running.md); the figures by `scripts/plot_results.py` into `data/plots/`
(git-ignored, like all data — the image links below work on a machine that has run it).

Steps 7–10 of ours were run again on 2026-11-27, at f7027c8, with the margin taken per vertex
from what a stream has seen; the baselines were not rerun. All 18 083 160 pairs are again
within (1 + ε)δ and within 2κ − 2, and every share and median below moves by at most a point.
The write-up's figures are `data/plots/writeup/01`–`04`.

Step 11, ours searched to each baseline's size and compared on error, was added the same day at
ε = 0.1: *At the same size*, below, and `data/plots/writeup/05_fixed-size`.

## The question, asked five ways

Ours takes an error bound δ and guarantees a Fréchet distance of at most (1 + ε)δ and at most
2κ(δ) − 2 vertices, κ(δ) the fewest any simplification within δ can have. The baselines take a
size or a threshold. So the comparison fixes one quantity and compares the other, both ways
([comparison.md](comparison.md)), and for each way picks δ one of two ways
([comparison.md](comparison.md), *Which δ*):

| step | fixed | baseline | ours | so ours is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **7**, fixed rate | N / 2^m vertices, m = 2…6 | run at that budget; its error d* measured | δ = d*/(1+ε) | within d* |
| **8**, fixed error | T = f · bounding-box diagonal, f = 0.0003…0.03 | the fewest vertices its knob was found to reach within T | δ = T/(1+ε) | within T |
| **9**, fixed rate | as step 7 | as step 7 | δ = d* | within (1+ε)d*, ≤ 2 · the baseline − 2 |
| **10**, fixed error | as step 8 | as step 8 | δ = T | within (1+ε)T, ≤ 2 · the baseline − 2 |
| **11**, fixed size | the baseline's vertices, as step 7 | as step 7 | δ bisected until ours fits in them, ε = 0.1 | no larger than the baseline; its error compared |

**Steps 7 and 8 are the comparison**: both sides within the same error, sizes compared. **Steps
9 and 10 test the size guarantee**: the baseline is within δ, so its size bounds κ(δ), and
Theorem 3 reads as ours ≤ 2 · the baseline's size − 2 in every pair. They let ours take up to
(1 + ε) times the baseline's error, and it does.

Each pair gives a **size ratio**, ours' vertices over the baseline's, in percent: under 100
ours is smaller. 857 456 pairs per ε in steps 7 and 9 (four baselines × five rates, fewer where
a budget is under a baseline's floor); 950 860 per ε in steps 8 and 10 (four baselines × five
levels, less DOTS on 32 trajectories). Five ε each, 0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05: 18 083 160 pairs.

## The guarantees, checked

- **Error**: ours within (1 + ε)δ in **all 18 083 160 pairs**, decided exactly by
  `Frechet::within`. In steps 7 and 8 that is within the baseline's error or T: 0 over.
- **Size**: ours ≤ 2κ − 2 wherever a curve on disk bounds κ, **0 over** in all four steps. In
  steps 9 and 10 the baseline itself bounds κ, so the check covers every one of their 9 041 580
  pairs: ours never needs more than twice the baseline's vertices less 2. In steps 7 and 8 κ is
  bounded only where some baseline is within ours' δ (92–95% of step 7's pairs).

Neither held on the first corpus runs. How they broke, and why they no longer can, is at the
end: *Arithmetic*.

## Headline: the same error (steps 7 and 8)

Share of pairs where ours is smaller / the same size / larger, pooled over the five levels,
ε = 0.1:

| baseline | fixed rate (7) | fixed error (8) |
|---|---|---|
| DPn | 55 / 14 / 30 | 51 / 20 / 29 |
| SQUISH | 98 / 1 / 1 | 97 / 2 / 1 |
| SQUISH-E | 95 / 2 / 3 | 97 / 2 / 1 |
| DOTS | 81 / 12 / 8 | 80 / 13 / 7 |

- **Against SQUISH and SQUISH-E ours wins almost always and by a lot**: a median of 11–22%
  of SQUISH's vertices at a fixed rate and 18–44% at a fixed error; 32–44% and 30–44% of
  SQUISH-E's.
- **Against DOTS, it wins four pairs in five**: a median of 56–75% of DOTS' vertices at a
  fixed rate, 61–83% at a fixed error, the gap closing as the error grows.
- **Against Douglas–Peucker it is even**: the median ratio is 90–100%, ours ahead at the
  fine end (smaller in 78% of pairs at m2, 63% at f0.0003) and tied at the coarse end, where
  up to 36% of the pairs are exactly the same size.
- **Both protocols agree.** The rankings, the medians and the trends with level are the same
  whether the size or the error is held fixed, which is the strongest evidence that none of
  this is an artefact of how either was set up.

## At δ = the error (steps 9 and 10)

The same table with ours at δ = d* or T:

| baseline | fixed rate (9) | fixed error (10) |
|---|---|---|
| DPn | 64 / 14 / 22 | 59 / 19 / 21 |
| SQUISH | 99 / 1 / 1 | 97 / 2 / 1 |
| SQUISH-E | 96 / 2 / 2 | 97 / 2 / 1 |
| DOTS | 83 / 11 / 6 | 82 / 12 / 5 |

- **What it buys**: the median ratios fall by 0–8 points, the most against DPn and DOTS
  (DPn at m2, 90.0% → 84.8%; DOTS at m6, 75.0% → 66.7%). Against DPn ours goes from even to
  ahead.
- **What it costs**: ours ends over the baseline's error in 92% of step 9's pairs and 89% of
  step 10's, at a median 1.07–1.10 times it (ε = 0.1). The extra size is bought with error, so
  steps 9–10 are not a fairer comparison than 7–8 — only the one where the size guarantee
  speaks about the baseline.
- **ε acts the other way round.** At δ = error/(1 + ε) a smaller ε makes ours a little
  smaller, as δ comes closer to the error: against DPn at m2, 94.6% of its size at ε = 0.5,
  89.2% at 0.05. At δ = error a larger ε does, as it buys error: 73.5% at 0.5, 86.5% at 0.05.
  The two meet as ε goes to 0; ε = 0.5 at δ = error is the largest compression and the largest
  error, 1.5 times the baseline's.

Step 7's baseline outputs, with ours given as many vertices: a bisection on δ, to 10⁻⁹ of the

(1 + ε)δ in all of them, never larger than the baseline, and over 2κ − 2 in none.

  saves no vertex.
- **At DOTS' size ours has half its error** (median 50%), and at SQUISH-E's and SQUISH's a
  median 21% and 7%.
- **By dataset**, at m4, the same places as step 7: against DPn ours is closer on DanceTrack

## Per baseline

Over all five ε, at the same error (steps 7 and 8).

**Douglas–Peucker (DPn).** The strongest baseline, and the only one that matches ours. At a
fixed rate the median ratio is 89–95% at m2 and exactly 100% from m4 on; at a fixed error,
94–99% at f0.0003 and 100% from f0.01. The ties are real: about 14% (fixed rate) and 20%
(fixed error) of pairs come out the same size. DPn sees the whole trajectory; ours sees it
one vertex at a time.

**SQUISH.** The weakest. At a fixed rate ours needs a median 11–12% of its vertices at m2 and
22–23% at m6, smaller in 97–99% of pairs. At a fixed error the median is 44–47% at f0.0003 and
18–24% from f0.003 on — and read the tight end with the caveat on fallbacks below.

**SQUISH-E.** Close behind: medians of 32–49% at a fixed rate, 30–47% at a fixed error,
ours smaller in 88–97%.

**DOTS.** The closest of the SED-based three: medians of 55–89%, ours smaller in 55–92% of
pairs, both falling with the level, the closest at the coarsest error.

## What to say alongside these numbers

- **Ours places vertices that are not input points; every baseline keeps a subsequence.** It
  is solving a strictly easier size problem, and that belongs in the caption.
- **Nobody else optimises Fréchet distance.** SQUISH, SQUISH-E and DOTS minimise synchronous
  Euclidean distance, DPn perpendicular distance. Scoring all of them under ours' objective
  is the right primary metric, and an unfair race for the SED-based three.
- **At tight error targets SQUISH and SQUISH-E often cannot meet T at all.** At f = 0.0003 no
  setting of theirs is within T on 27% of trajectories (27.1% SQUISH, 27.6% SQUISH-E; 15–17%
  at f0.001, 1–2% at f0.03), and they are scored as keeping the whole input. DPn falls back
  in 0.3–2.5%, DOTS never. Those pairs are wins for ours that say more about the baseline's
  knob than about ours.
- **A baseline's best at a fixed error is the fewest vertices found, not proven fewest.**
  None of the four is monotone in its knob, so the bisection stops at a boundary: one vertex
  fewer at that knob breaks T.
- **Ours spends its whole error budget; the baselines do not.** In step 8 ours' median
  distance is 0.99–1.00·T at every level (ε = 0.1); DPn's is 0.71–0.94·T, DOTS'
  0.75–0.90·T, SQUISH's 0.54–0.82·T and SQUISH-E's 0.19–0.76·T, the low ends at f0.0003 where
  those two often keep everything. That is why `05_median_distance_ratio.png` sits above 100%
  at a fixed error: the comparison is of size at the same budget, and the distance ratio says
  how much of it each side used. At a fixed rate ours is held to the baseline's own d*, so the
  distance ratio sits at 98–100% by construction.
- **Steps 9–10's sizes come with (1 + ε) times the error.** Quote them for the guarantee, or
  beside their error, never as the headline.
- **DOTS is missing on 32 trajectories at a fixed error** (the 31 over 30 000 points and
  mopsi-003388, whose DOTS search does not finish in minutes) and on geolife-013552 at a
  fixed rate.

## Arithmetic: what broke, and why it no longer can

Ours' guarantees are theorems in exact arithmetic; the code runs in doubles. Three things broke
on the first corpus runs, every one of them caught by the exact checks above, not by
inspection.

1. **`geometry::closest` took a far query point as inside a sliver.** Step 7 at ε = 0.5: 11 of
   857 456 pairs over (1 + ε)δ, up to 2×, in two Mopsi and three MOT20 trajectories. A spike
   to where two m-gons meet corner to corner left a shadow 10⁻¹⁰δ wide; `closest` skipped every
   one of its edges as degenerate, measuring "degenerate" against the distance to the query
   point. Now an edge is skipped only when ulps long against its own coordinates. 0 over since.
   That held near the first vertex only: far from it such a shadow has every edge a few ulps of
   its coordinates long, all were skipped, and q was still inside. A synthetic spike 10–10⁶δ
   from the first vertex, its gap just over `run`'s margin, went over in 1 532 of 4 368 runs, at
   1.72δ against 1.5δ; none of the corpus runs did. Since 2026-11-26 q is kept only when it is
   also within the shadow's perimeter of its nearest boundary point, which a point inside is.
2. **Under the precision margin, `run` returned the input whole.** Where a baseline's error is
   0 within measurement — a budget that dropped only exact repeats of a GPS fix — step 7 asked
   ours for a δ of a few ulps of the coordinates, below the margin `run` keeps for rounding, and
   it returned every point: 55 pairs in 13 trajectories over 2κ − 2. Now it drops the exact
   repeats, which is exact.
3. **The margin was charged to the wrong coordinates.** It was 16 ulps of the largest absolute
   coordinate: 2.3·10⁻⁸ ft at NGSIM's State Plane 6.45·10⁶ ft. Step 9 found one more pair:
   ngsim-002153, whose DPn drops runs of fixes collinear to the bit at error 0, so δ = 3.4·10⁻⁹
   ft, and ours fell back with 170 vertices against 2 · 61 − 2. But `run` computes relative to
   the first vertex, where its error is ulps of the curve's *extent*; only writing a point back
   costs half an ulp of the absolute coordinates. The margin is now that,
   `DBL_EPSILON · (max|coord| + 2δ) + 64 · DBL_EPSILON · extent` — 1.4·10⁻⁹ ft on NGSIM,
   1.4·10⁻¹⁴ degrees on Mopsi, 16 and 15 times lower — and ours stabs those runs.

**What is still under the floor**: 98 pairs per ε in steps 7 and 9 (168–208 and 164 under the
old margin), all in 7 trajectories that barely move — extents of 0.03–0.9 ft on NGSIM
Lankershim, 7·10⁻⁶ degrees on GeoLife — where every baseline's error is 0 within measurement.
There no new point can be written within δ; ours keeps their 3 to 7 distinct points, within
both bounds. Steps 8 and 10 never come near the floor.

**Why it should not recur:**

- **Every pair is checked, exactly, every run.** `within_bound` by `Frechet::within` in every
  result; the size bound against the baseline itself in steps 9–10. A failure is a count in
  step 12's check table, not a sample.
- **Each fix has a regression test that failed before it**, in
  `tests/test_greedy_stabbing.cpp`: the corner-to-corner spike; repeats below the floor; the
  collinear runs a few ulps above it; and the bound just above the floor at State Plane
  coordinates, all four stabbers.
- **The margin is derived, not tuned**: each term is the rounding one operation can do, so it
  stays right on coordinates of any size, and the floor sits where no new point can be
  written, one to two ulps of the coordinates.
- **Steps 9 and 10 stay in the runbook.** They are what found the third, and the only place
  the size bound is checked in every pair.
