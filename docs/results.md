# Results: ours against the four baselines

Measured 2026-11-24 on the whole corpus, 47 916 trajectories in nine datasets, with
`greedy-stabbing-shared` (Algorithm 3), after both arithmetic fixes of that day (`geometry::closest`,
and the precision floor of `GreedyStabber::run`). Every number here is regenerated by steps
7–10 of [running.md](running.md); the figures by `scripts/plot_results.py` into `data/plots/`
(git-ignored, like all data — the image links below work on a machine that has run it).

Steps 7 and 8 of ours were run again on 2026-11-27, at f7027c8, with the margin taken per vertex
from what a stream has seen; the baselines were not rerun. Every pair is again within (1 + ε)δ
and within 2κ − 2, and every share and median below moves by at most a point. Step 9, ours
searched to each baseline's size and compared on error, was added the same day at ε = 0.1. The
write-up's figures are `data/plots/writeup/01`–`04`.

## The question, asked three ways

Ours takes an error bound δ and guarantees a Fréchet distance of at most (1 + ε)δ and at most
2κ(δ) − 2 vertices, κ(δ) the fewest any simplification within δ can have. The baselines take a
size or a threshold. So each baseline is run at a fixed compression rate, its error d\* is
measured, and ours is compared with it three ways ([comparison.md](comparison.md)):

| step | baseline | ours | so ours is | compared |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **7**, fixed rate | N / 2^m vertices, m = 2…6; its error d\* measured | δ = d\*/(1+ε) | within d\* | size |
| **8**, fixed rate, δ = d\* | as step 7 | δ = d\* | within (1+ε)d\*, ≤ 2 · the baseline − 2 | size |
| **9**, fixed size | as step 7 | δ bisected until ours fits in the baseline's vertices, ε = 0.1 | no larger than the baseline | error |

**Step 7 is the comparison**: both sides within the same error, sizes compared. **Step 8 tests
the size guarantee**: the baseline is within δ, so its size bounds κ(δ), and Theorem 3 reads as
ours ≤ 2 · the baseline's size − 2 in every pair. It lets ours take up to (1 + ε) times the
baseline's error, and it does. **Step 9 reads step 7 the other way round**: at the same size,
whose error is smaller.

Each pair gives a **size ratio**, ours' vertices over the baseline's, in percent: under 100
ours is smaller. 857 456 pairs per ε in each step (four baselines × five rates, fewer where a
budget is under a baseline's floor); five ε in steps 7 and 8, 0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, and
ε = 0.1 in step 9: 9 432 016 pairs.

## The guarantees, checked

- **Error**: ours within (1 + ε)δ in **all 9 432 016 pairs**, decided exactly by
  `Frechet::within`. In step 7 that is within the baseline's error: 0 over.
- **Size**: ours ≤ 2κ − 2 wherever a curve on disk bounds κ, **0 over** in all three steps. In
  step 8 the baseline itself bounds κ, so the check covers every one of its 4 287 280 pairs:
  ours never needs more than twice the baseline's vertices less 2. In steps 7 and 9 κ is
  bounded only where some baseline is within ours' δ (92–95% of step 7's pairs).

Neither held on the first corpus runs. How they broke, and why they no longer can, is at the
end: *Arithmetic*.

## Headline: the same error (step 7)

Share of pairs where ours is smaller / the same size / larger, pooled over the five rates,
ε = 0.1:

| baseline | fixed rate (7) |
|---|---|
| DPn | 55 / 14 / 30 |
| SQUISH | 98 / 1 / 1 |
| SQUISH-E | 95 / 2 / 3 |
| DOTS | 81 / 12 / 8 |

- **Against SQUISH and SQUISH-E ours wins almost always and by a lot**: a median of 11–22%
  of SQUISH's vertices and 32–44% of SQUISH-E's.
- **Against DOTS, it wins four pairs in five**: a median of 56–75% of DOTS' vertices, the gap
  closing as the rate coarsens.
- **Against Douglas–Peucker it is even**: the median ratio is 90–100%, ours ahead at the fine
  end (smaller in 68–79% of pairs at m2) and tied at the coarse end, where up to 26% of the

## At δ = d\* (step 8)

The same table with ours at δ = d\*:

| baseline | fixed rate, δ = d\* (8) |
|---|---|
| DPn | 64 / 14 / 22 |
| SQUISH | 99 / 1 / 1 |
| SQUISH-E | 96 / 2 / 2 |
| DOTS | 83 / 11 / 6 |

- **What it buys**: the median ratios fall by 0–8 points, the most against DPn and DOTS
  (DPn at m2, 90.0% → 84.8%; DOTS at m6, 75.0% → 66.7%). Against DPn ours goes from even to
  ahead.
- **What it costs**: ours ends over the baseline's error in 92% of the pairs, at a median
  1.07–1.10 times it (ε = 0.1). The extra size is bought with error, so step 8 is not a fairer
  comparison than step 7 — only the one where the size guarantee speaks about the baseline.
- **ε acts the other way round.** At δ = d\*/(1 + ε) a smaller ε makes ours a little
  smaller, as δ comes closer to d\*: against DPn at m2, 94.6% of its size at ε = 0.5,
  89.2% at 0.05. At δ = d\* a larger ε does, as it buys error: 73.5% at 0.5, 86.5% at 0.05.
  The two meet as ε goes to 0; ε = 0.5 at δ = d\* is the largest compression and the largest
  error, 1.5 times the baseline's.

## At the same size (step 9)

Step 7's baseline outputs, with ours given as many vertices: a bisection on δ, to 10⁻⁹ of the
diagonal, finds where ours first fits in the baseline's count, and ours' measured Fréchet
distance is compared with the baseline's d\*. 857 456 pairs at ε = 0.1. Ours is within
(1 + ε)δ in all of them, never larger than the baseline, and over 2κ − 2 in none.

Share of pairs where ours is closer / as close / farther, pooled over m2–m6, and the median
**distance ratio**, ours' over the baseline's, at each m:

| baseline | closer / same / farther | median, m2 … m6 |
|---|---|---|
| DPn | 69.4 / 0.8 / 29.8 | 82, 85, 92, 92, 85 |
| SQUISH | 98.6 / 0.7 / 0.8 | 3, 5, 8, 9, 11 |
| SQUISH-E | 96.7 / 0.6 / 2.8 | 16, 18, 20, 25, 31 |
| DOTS | 91.8 / 0.7 / 7.5 | 42, 47, 51, 53, 54 |

- **It is step 7 read the other way round, and it agrees.** The ranking is the same, and so is
  the share where ours loses to DPn, 30%. What changes is DPn's ties: step 7's 14% same-size
  pairs are mostly closer here, as a size is a whole number and a slightly smaller error often
  saves no vertex.
- **At DOTS' size ours has half its error** (median 50%), and at SQUISH-E's and SQUISH's a
  median 21% and 7%.
- **By dataset**, at m4, the same places as step 7: against DPn ours is closer on DanceTrack
  (median 75%, 95% of pairs) and farther on NGSIM I-80, US-101 and MOT20 (118–127%).
- **Ours uses its whole bound** — its measured distance is a median 1.0000 of (1 + ε)δ — and it
  ends strictly under the baseline's size in 43–48% of pairs, as its size jumps past k between
  two δ a tolerance apart. Neither favours it.
- **The floor**: in 5 925 pairs the baseline is at 0 within measurement; ours is too in 5 839,
  counted as ties, which is where the 0.6–0.8% "same" comes from.

`scripts/ratio_buckets.py` counts these pairs that way for every comparison, which moved the
distance tables of steps 7 and 8 by at most 1.6 points and their size tables not at all.

## Per baseline

Over all five ε, at the same error (step 7).

**Douglas–Peucker (DPn).** The strongest baseline, and the only one that matches ours. The
median ratio is 89–95% at m2 and exactly 100% from m4 on. The ties are real: about 14% of
pairs come out the same size. DPn sees the whole trajectory; ours sees it one vertex at a time.

**SQUISH.** The weakest. Ours needs a median 11–12% of its vertices at m2 and 22–23% at m6,
smaller in 97–99% of pairs.

**SQUISH-E.** Close behind: medians of 32–49%, ours smaller in 88–97%.

**DOTS.** The closest of the SED-based three: medians of 55–80%, ours smaller in 62–92% of
pairs, both falling with the rate, the closest at the coarsest.

## What to say alongside these numbers

- **Ours places vertices that are not input points; every baseline keeps a subsequence.** It
  is solving a strictly easier size problem, and that belongs in the caption.
- **Nobody else optimises Fréchet distance.** SQUISH, SQUISH-E and DOTS minimise synchronous
  Euclidean distance, DPn perpendicular distance. Scoring all of them under ours' objective
  is the right primary metric, and an unfair race for the SED-based three.
- **Step 8's sizes come with (1 + ε) times the error.** Quote them for the guarantee, or
  beside their error, never as the headline.
- **Step 9 searches ours**, rerunning it on the whole trajectory, so there ours is not online,
  as DOTS is not with its own search.
- **DOTS is missing on geolife-013552**, whose DOTS search does not finish in minutes.

## Arithmetic: what broke, and why it no longer can

Ours' guarantees are theorems in exact arithmetic; the code runs in doubles. Three things broke
on the first corpus runs, every one of them caught by the exact checks above, not by
inspection.

1. **`geometry::closest` took a far query point as inside a sliver.** Step 7 at ε = 0.5: 11 of
   857 456 pairs over (1 + ε)δ, up to 2×, in two Mopsi and three MOT20 trajectories. A spike
   to where two m-gons meet corner to corner left a shadow 10⁻¹⁰δ wide; `closest` skipped every
   one of its edges as degenerate, measuring "degenerate" against the distance to the query
   point. Now an edge is skipped only when ulps long against its own coordinates. 0 over since.
   That held near the first vertex only: far from it such a shadow has every edge a few ulps of
   its coordinates long, all were skipped, and q was still inside. A synthetic spike 10–10⁶δ
   from the first vertex, its gap just over `run`'s margin, went over in 1 532 of 4 368 runs, at
   1.72δ against 1.5δ; none of the corpus runs did. Since 2026-11-26 q is kept only when it is
   also within the shadow's perimeter of its nearest boundary point, which a point inside is.
2. **Under the precision margin, `run` returned the input whole.** Where a baseline's error is
   0 within measurement — a budget that dropped only exact repeats of a GPS fix — step 7 asked
   ours for a δ of a few ulps of the coordinates, below the margin `run` keeps for rounding, and
   it returned every point: 55 pairs in 13 trajectories over 2κ − 2. Now it drops the exact
   repeats, which is exact.
3. **The margin was charged to the wrong coordinates.** It was 16 ulps of the largest absolute
   coordinate: 2.3·10⁻⁸ ft at NGSIM's State Plane 6.45·10⁶ ft. Step 8 found one more pair:
   ngsim-002153, whose DPn drops runs of fixes collinear to the bit at error 0, so δ = 3.4·10⁻⁹
   ft, and ours fell back with 170 vertices against 2 · 61 − 2. But `run` computes relative to
   the first vertex, where its error is ulps of the curve's *extent*; only writing a point back
   costs half an ulp of the absolute coordinates. The margin is now that,
   `DBL_EPSILON · (max|coord| + 2δ) + 64 · DBL_EPSILON · extent` — 1.4·10⁻⁹ ft on NGSIM,
   1.4·10⁻¹⁴ degrees on Mopsi, 16 and 15 times lower — and ours stabs those runs.

**What is still under the floor**: 98 pairs per ε in steps 7 and 8 (168–208 and 164 under the
old margin), all in 7 trajectories that barely move — extents of 0.03–0.9 ft on NGSIM
Lankershim, 7·10⁻⁶ degrees on GeoLife — where every baseline's error is 0 within measurement.
There no new point can be written within δ; ours keeps their 3 to 7 distinct points, within
both bounds.

**Why it should not recur:**

- **Every pair is checked, exactly, every run.** `within_bound` by `Frechet::within` in every
  result; the size bound against the baseline itself in step 8. A failure is a count in
  step 10's check table, not a sample.
- **Each fix has a regression test that failed before it**, in
  `tests/test_greedy_stabbing.cpp`: the corner-to-corner spike; repeats below the floor; the
  collinear runs a few ulps above it; and the bound just above the floor at State Plane
  coordinates, all four stabbers.
- **The margin is derived, not tuned**: each term is the rounding one operation can do, so it
  stays right on coordinates of any size, and the floor sits where no new point can be
  written, one to two ulps of the coordinates.
- **Step 8 stays in the runbook.** It is what found the third, and the only place the size
  bound is checked in every pair.
