# Experiment 4: fixed Fréchet distance, ours at δ = T

## The experiment

Step 10 of [running.md](../running.md), experiment 4 of the write-up's Appendix C.

- **The baselines' side is experiment 2's**, unchanged and not rerun:
  - every trajectory with extent gets five targets T = f · its bounding-box diagonal, for
    f ∈ {0.0003, 0.001, 0.003, 0.01, 0.03};
  - Douglas–Peucker (DPn), SQUISH, SQUISH-E and DOTS each get the fewest vertices found
    within T, by binary search on their own parameter, decided exactly.
- **Ours** is `greedy-stabbing-shared` (Algorithm 3 of the write-up), now at **δ = T** rather
  than T/(1 + ε). It is therefore within (1 + ε)T, and may spend up to ε · T more error than
  the baseline's budget. It is run for each ε ∈ {0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05}, and every output
  is checked exactly against (1 + ε)δ.

**A pair** is one trajectory, one baseline, one target f and one ε. Its **size ratio** is ours'
vertices over the baseline's, in percent.

## Where the data is

- `data/target-matched-unscaled/greedy-stabbing-shared/eps<ε>/summary/<dataset>/<name>.json`:
  one per trajectory and ε, written by `ssk_target_match --unscaled-delta` (step 10). It has the
  shape of experiment 2's: each entry of `pairs` has `algorithm`, `level`, `target`,
  `baseline.output_size`, `baseline.input`, `ours.delta` (= T), `ours.output_size`,
  `ours.within_bound` and `ours.within_target`. Its `delta_rule` is `T`.
- Ours' curves are under `results/` beside the summaries; the baselines' are in
  `data/target-simplified/`.
- The tables below are written by `scripts/ratio_percentiles.py`, with the ratios of
  `scripts/ratio_buckets.py` (step 12).

## How to read the tables

There is one table per ε and target f, with a row per baseline: the percentiles of the size
ratio over that row's pairs, pooled over the nine datasets. A percentile is the nearest rank:
pK is the smallest ratio with at least K% of the pairs at or below it. **worst** is the largest
ratio over the row's pairs.

**The worst column is the check of this experiment.** The baseline is within T = δ, so its size
bounds κ(δ), and Theorem 3 gives ours ≤ 2 · the baseline's size − 2 in every pair. Worst must
therefore be below 200 in every row.

## The goal

This experiment **tests the size guarantee at a fixed error**, as experiment 3 does at a fixed
rate. Here ours' size bound is against κ(T), the optimum at the baseline's own budget, rather
than κ(T/(1 + ε)) as in experiment 2. Every baseline output is a simplification within T, so
it bounds κ(T), and the bound can be read against it pair by pair.

## Caveats

- **This is not a fairer size comparison than experiment 2.** Ours ends over T in 89% of the
  pairs at every ε (843 912 of 950 860 at ε = 0.1), as it takes the extra error it is allowed.
  Read its sizes beside experiment 2's.
- **Whole-input baselines.** Where no setting is within T, the baseline is counted with the
  whole input:
  - SQUISH-E in 10.9% of its pairs, SQUISH in 11.8% and DPn in 1.2%, mostly at f = 0.0003;
  - there the size bound holds trivially, and the ratio says more about the baseline's
    parameters than about ours.
- **The baselines' searches find a boundary, not a minimum**, since none is monotone in its
  parameter. A smaller simplification within T may exist, so the true κ(T) can be below the
  baseline's size.
- **The size bound is a theorem about κ**, and the baseline is only one simplification within
  T, so a ratio far under 200% says nothing about how tight the bound is against κ itself.
- **The other caveats are experiment 2's:**
  - DOTS has 32 fewer pairs per target (31 trajectories over 30 000 vertices and one Mopsi
    trajectory);
  - 365 trajectories without extent have no targets;
  - the baselines keep subsequences and minimise other distances;
  - DOTS is searched.
