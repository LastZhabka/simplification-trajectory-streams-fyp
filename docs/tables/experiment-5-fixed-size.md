# Experiment 5: fixed size, ours searched to the baseline's size

## The experiment

Step 11 of [running.md](../running.md), experiment 5 of the write-up's Appendix C.

- **The baselines' side is experiment 1's**, unchanged and not rerun:
  - every trajectory of the corpus (47 916 trajectories, 47 102 992 vertices) is simplified by
    Douglas–Peucker (DPn), SQUISH, SQUISH-E and DOTS to k = ⌈N/2^m⌉ vertices, m = 2, …, 6;
  - SQUISH-E is at most k, and DOTS reaches k by a search on its threshold;
  - the Fréchet distance d\* of each output is measured to 10⁻⁹ of the bounding-box diagonal.
- **Ours** is `greedy-stabbing-shared` (Algorithm 3 of the write-up), at **ε = 0.1 only**.
  It gets the number of vertices the baseline actually kept, k or fewer.
  - **The search.** A binary search on δ keeps an interval [lo, hi], with ours at hi within
    the baseline's size and at lo not. It starts from lo = 0 and hi = the bounding-box
    diagonal, where every segment in the box is within δ, so κ = 2 and ours has 2 vertices.
    It halves the interval until it is 10⁻⁹ of the diagonal.
  - **The measurement.** Ours at hi is measured as the baselines are, and checked exactly
    against (1 + ε)·hi.
  - **Sharing.** The search depends only on the trajectory and the size, so the baselines of
    one trajectory share it: two outputs of the same size get the same run of ours.

**A pair** is one trajectory, one baseline and one m. Its **distance ratio** is ours' measured
Fréchet distance over the baseline's d\*, in percent. Under 100, ours is closer to the
trajectory with at most as many vertices.

## Where the data is

- `data/size-matched/greedy-stabbing-shared/eps0.1/summary/<dataset>/<name>.json`: one per
  trajectory, written by `ssk_size_match` (step 11).
  - Each entry of `pairs` has `algorithm`, `m`, `baseline.output_size`, `baseline.distance`
    (d\*) and `baseline.tolerance`.
  - It also has `ours.delta` (the δ found), `ours.output_size`, `ours.distance`,
    `ours.tolerance`, `ours.within_bound` and `ours.algorithm_runs` (runs of ours the search
    made).
  - Its `delta_rule` is `size`.
- Ours' curves are beside the summaries, under `<algorithm>/<dataset>/m<m>/`, each with the
  baseline document it was fitted to under `matched`.
- The tables below are written by `scripts/ratio_percentiles.py`, with the distance ratio of
  `scripts/ratio_buckets.py` (step 12).

## How to read the tables

There is one table per m, with a row per baseline: the percentiles of the distance ratio over
that row's pairs, pooled over the nine datasets. A percentile is the nearest rank: pK is the
smallest ratio with at least K% of the pairs at or below it. **worst** is the largest ratio,
the single pair where ours is farthest from the trajectory relative to the baseline. Lower is
better for ours in every column.

## The goal

This experiment reads experiment 1's baseline outputs the other way round. It asks **with the
vertices the baseline kept, how close does ours get?**, rather than how many vertices ours
needs for the baseline's error. Sizes are whole numbers, so in experiment 1 an error a little
below the baseline's often saves no vertex, and the pair is a tie. Here such an advantage shows
in the distance, so the two experiments together give both sides of the trade-off.

## Caveats

- **Ours is not online here.** The search reruns it on the whole trajectory, 19 times per pair
  on average, as DOTS' search reruns DOTS. The numbers describe the simplification, not a
  streaming run.
- **The search finds a boundary, not a minimum.** Ours' size is not guaranteed to fall as δ
  grows, so hi is the smallest δ found at which ours fits, not the smallest possible.
- **Ours ends at the baseline's size or under it.** Its size can jump past k between two δ a
  tolerance apart, so it keeps strictly fewer vertices than the baseline in 43–48% of the
  pairs. That never favours ours.
- **Ours uses its whole bound.** Its measured distance is a median 1.0000 of (1 + ε)δ, so the
  ratio measures ours' actual error, not the δ it was asked for.
- **At a fixed size, ours is only guaranteed the optimum's error at about half the size.**
  This is what the p99 and worst columns show.
  - Ours has at most 2κ(δ) − 2 vertices, so it fits in k once κ(δ) ≤ (k + 2)/2. The δ it may
    need is the optimum's error with (k + 2)/2 vertices, not with k.
  - Where the error falls steeply between those two sizes, ours is far from a baseline that is
    good at k. At k = 3 that means ours may need δ at which a single segment fits.
  - 468 pairs are over 1 000%, and 387 of them have a budget of 3 to 5 vertices.
  - **The largest, 10⁸–10¹⁰%, are 86 pairs where the baseline is exact.** Its d\* is 0 within
    measurement, as k of its points reproduce the track, and ours' is not. These are tracks of
    14 to 2 880 points: 42 on MOT17, 18 on Lankershim, 12 on Mopsi, 11 on MOT20 and 3 on
    Peachtree.
- **The precision floor.** In 5 925 pairs, d\* is within its tolerance: the baseline is at
  error 0 within measurement, having dropped exact repeats or points collinear to the bit.
  - Ours is within the two tolerances in 5 839 of them. Those count as ties (100), since a
    ratio of two numbers below the tolerance is noise.
  - The other 86 are the exact baselines of the previous item, and they keep their ratio.
- **ε = 0.1 only.** Experiments 1–4 show that ε moves the sizes by a few points.
- **The baselines' caveats are experiment 1's:**
  - their outputs are subsequences;
  - none minimises the Fréchet distance;
  - SQUISH-E and DOTS can land under k, and ours then gets their smaller size;
  - DOTS is searched and missing on one GeoLife trajectory;
  - short trajectories drop out of the deep rates.
