# Experiment 1: fixed compression rate, ours within the baseline's error

## The experiment

Step 7 of [running.md](../running.md), experiment 1 of the write-up's Appendix C.

- **Corpus.** Every trajectory of the nine datasets: 47 916 trajectories, 47 102 992 vertices
  (GeoLife, Mopsi, NGSIM US-101, I-80, Lankershim and Peachtree, MOT17, MOT20, DanceTrack),
  each in its own units.
- **The baselines** are Douglas–Peucker (psimpl's point-count variant DPn), SQUISH, SQUISH-E and
  DOTS ([comparison.md](../comparison.md)). Each simplifies every trajectory of N points to a
  budget of k = ⌈N/2^m⌉ vertices, for m = 2, …, 6:
  - DPn and SQUISH take k directly and return exactly k vertices.
  - SQUISH-E runs with `cmp_ratio` = 2^m and `sed_error` = 0, and returns at most k, usually
    one fewer.
  - DOTS has no size parameter, so its `lssd_threshold` is found by binary search to reach k.
- **The baseline's error.** Its Fréchet distance d\* to the trajectory is measured by binary
  search on the exact decision procedure, to 10⁻⁹ of the trajectory's bounding-box diagonal
  (step 6).
- **Ours** is `greedy-stabbing-shared` (Algorithm 3 of the write-up). It runs at
  δ = d\*/(1 + ε), so its guarantee of (1 + ε)δ puts it within d\*, the baseline's own error.
  It is run for each ε ∈ {0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05}, and every output is checked exactly
  against (1 + ε)δ.

**A pair** is one trajectory, one baseline, one m and one ε. Its **size ratio** is ours'
vertices over the baseline's, in percent. Under 100, ours is smaller at the same error.

## Where the data is

- `data/error-matched/greedy-stabbing-shared/eps<ε>/summary/<dataset>/<name>.json`: one per
  trajectory and ε, written by `ssk_match` (step 7). Each entry of `pairs` has `algorithm`,
  `m`, `baseline.output_size`, `baseline.distance` (d\*), `ours.delta`, `ours.output_size` and
  `ours.within_bound`.
- Ours' simplified curves are beside the summaries, under
  `data/error-matched/greedy-stabbing-shared/eps<ε>/<algorithm>/<dataset>/m<m>/`. The
  baselines' curves are in `data/simplified-trajectories/`, and their distances in
  `data/frechet-distances/`.
- The tables below are written by `scripts/ratio_percentiles.py`, with the ratios of
  `scripts/ratio_buckets.py` (step 12).

## How to read the tables

There is one table per ε and m, with a row per baseline: the percentiles of the size ratio over
that row's pairs, pooled over the nine datasets. A percentile is the nearest rank: pK is the
smallest ratio with at least K% of the pairs at or below it. **worst** is the largest ratio, the
single pair where ours did worst against that baseline. Lower is better for ours in every
column. p50 is the median of [ratio-buckets.md](../../data/ratio-buckets.md) and
[results.md](../results.md).

## The goal

This experiment answers **at the same error, how many vertices does each need?** The
baselines can be set by size but not by error, and ours by error but not by size, so each side
is driven by the quantity it can hit exactly. The comparison is then made on the other one,
with no search on ours' side. The percentiles show more than the median: how far ahead ours is
on most pairs (the low percentiles) and how bad its worst pairs are (p95, p99, worst).

## Caveats

- **Ours places new points**, while every baseline keeps a subsequence of the input. That is
  an easier problem for ours.
- **No baseline minimises the Fréchet distance.** DPn works with the distance to a line;
  SQUISH, SQUISH-E and DOTS with the synchronous Euclidean distance. All are scored on ours'
  objective.
- **d\* is whatever error the baseline happened to reach**, and ours is held within it.
  - Ours' size bound is against κ(d\*/(1 + ε)), the optimum at a stricter error than the
    baseline's, so nothing bounds the worst column by twice the baseline. That bound is what
    experiment 3 tests.
  - **The worst column** is 1 000–2 933% depending on ε (1 200% at ε = 0.1). At ε = 0.1, 193
    of the 197 pairs over 300% are on NGSIM Peachtree. There a baseline keeps 3 or 4 vertices,
    and ours, held a factor 1 + ε under that baseline's error, needs 30 or more. These tables
    cannot tell whether κ itself jumps just under d\* there, or ours is far from its bound.
  - A size ratio exactly 100 is common against DPn at the coarse rates. There, both are often
    at 2 or 3 vertices.
- **DOTS' size comes from a search** on its threshold, which costs it its online property. It
  has no pairs on one GeoLife trajectory, where the search did not finish.
- **Short trajectories drop out of the deep rates**, and not equally:
  - a rate is run only where its budget is at least 2 vertices for DPn and DOTS, 3 for
    SQUISH-E and 5 for SQUISH;
  - so the rows of one table do not cover the same trajectories (pair counts above).
- **SQUISH-E and DOTS can land under k.** That favours them here, since ours is compared with
  the size they reached, not with k.
- **The precision floor.** In 5 925 pairs per ε, d\* is within its tolerance, an error of 0
  within measurement: the budget only dropped exact repeats or runs collinear to the bit.
  Ours is then asked for a δ of a few ulps, and keeps the distinct points
  ([running.md](../running.md), step 7, *The precision floor*).
- **Pooled over nine datasets** of very different shapes. The tables per dataset are in
  `data/ratio-buckets.json`, and the medians per dataset in the write-up's Table
  `tab:cmp-datasets`.
