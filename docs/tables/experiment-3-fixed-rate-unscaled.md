# Experiment 3: fixed compression rate, ours at δ = d\*

## The experiment

Step 9 of [running.md](../running.md), experiment 3 of the write-up's Appendix C.

- **The baselines' side is experiment 1's**, unchanged and not rerun:
  - every trajectory of the corpus (47 916 trajectories, 47 102 992 vertices) is simplified by
    Douglas–Peucker (DPn), SQUISH, SQUISH-E and DOTS to k = ⌈N/2^m⌉ vertices, m = 2, …, 6;
  - SQUISH-E is at most k, and DOTS reaches k by a search on its threshold;
  - the Fréchet distance d\* of each output is measured to 10⁻⁹ of the bounding-box diagonal.
- **Ours** is `greedy-stabbing-shared` (Algorithm 3 of the write-up), now at **δ = d\***
  rather than d\*/(1 + ε). It is therefore within (1 + ε)d\*, and may spend up to ε · d\* more
  error than the baseline. It is run for each ε ∈ {0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05}, and every output
  is checked exactly against (1 + ε)δ.

**A pair** is one trajectory, one baseline, one m and one ε. Its **size ratio** is ours'
vertices over the baseline's, in percent.

## Where the data is

- `data/error-matched-unscaled/greedy-stabbing-shared/eps<ε>/summary/<dataset>/<name>.json`:
  one per trajectory and ε, written by `ssk_match --unscaled-delta` (step 9). It has the shape
  of experiment 1's: each entry of `pairs` has `algorithm`, `m`, `baseline.output_size`,
  `baseline.distance`, `ours.delta` (= d\*), `ours.output_size`, `ours.distance` and
  `ours.within_bound`. Its `delta_rule` is `d*`.
- Ours' curves are beside the summaries, under `<algorithm>/<dataset>/m<m>/`. The baselines'
  are in `data/simplified-trajectories/` and `data/frechet-distances/`.
- The tables below are written by `scripts/ratio_percentiles.py`, with the ratios of
  `scripts/ratio_buckets.py` (step 12).

## How to read the tables

There is one table per ε and m, with a row per baseline: the percentiles of the size ratio over
that row's pairs, pooled over the nine datasets. A percentile is the nearest rank: pK is the
smallest ratio with at least K% of the pairs at or below it. **worst** is the largest ratio over
the row's pairs.

**The worst column is the check of this experiment.** The baseline is within δ = d\*, so its
size bounds κ(δ), and Theorem 3 (ours has at most 2κ(δ) − 2 vertices) gives ours ≤ 2k − 2 in
every pair. The size ratio is therefore always under 200%, and worst must be below 200 in every
row.

## The goal

This experiment **tests the size guarantee against a real simplification.** Ours is
bicriteria: run at δ, it is within (1 + ε)δ and has at most 2κ(δ) − 2 vertices. No δ puts both
guarantees at the baseline's error:

- experiment 1 runs ours inside the error, and its size bound is against the optimum at a
  stricter error;
- this experiment runs ours at the error, so its size bound reads directly against the
  baseline.

The answer is in the worst column. The other columns show what the extra error buys in size,
compared with experiment 1.

## Caveats

- **This is not a fairer size comparison than experiment 1.** Ours really does use the extra
  error. It ends over the baseline's d\* in 92% of the pairs at every ε (791 257 of 857 456 at
  ε = 0.1), at a median 1.07–1.10 times it. Its sizes are bought with error, so read them
  beside experiment 1's.
- **ε acts the other way round from experiment 1.** Here a larger ε is more error and fewer
  vertices; in experiment 1 a smaller ε brings δ closer to d\*. The two meet as ε goes to 0.
- **The baselines' caveats are experiment 1's:**
  - their outputs are subsequences;
  - none minimises the Fréchet distance;
  - DOTS is searched and missing on one GeoLife trajectory;
  - short trajectories drop out of the deep rates at different budgets for each baseline.
- **The size bound is a theorem about κ**, and the baseline is only one simplification within
  δ. A ratio far under 200% does not mean the bound is loose against κ itself.
- **The precision floor.** Where d\* is within its tolerance (5 925 pairs per ε), ours is run
  at δ of a few ulps and keeps the distinct points, still within both bounds.
