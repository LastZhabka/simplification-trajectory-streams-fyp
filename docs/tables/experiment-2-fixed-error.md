# Experiment 2: fixed Fréchet distance, both within the same target

## The experiment

Step 8 of [running.md](../running.md), experiment 2 of the write-up's Appendix C.

- **Corpus.** Every trajectory of the nine datasets, 47 916 trajectories and 47 102 992
  vertices, each in its own units.
- **The targets.** Each trajectory gets five error targets T = f · the diagonal of its bounding
  box, for f ∈ {0.0003, 0.001, 0.003, 0.01, 0.03}. A fraction of the diagonal means the same in
  degrees, feet and pixels. The five are half a decade apart and span the medians of experiment
  1's d\*/diagonal.
- **The baselines** are Douglas–Peucker (DPn), SQUISH, SQUISH-E and DOTS. At each target, each
  gets the fewest vertices found within T, by binary search on its own parameter, with every
  step decided exactly by the Fréchet decision procedure:
  - on `count` for DPn;
  - on `buffer_size` for SQUISH;
  - on a budget k, run as `cmp_ratio` = ⌈(N − 1)/k⌉, for SQUISH-E;
  - on log(`lssd_threshold`) for DOTS.
- **Ours** is `greedy-stabbing-shared` (Algorithm 3 of the write-up). It runs at
  δ = T/(1 + ε), so it is within T by its guarantee, for each ε ∈ {0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05}.
  It does not depend on the baseline, so it runs once per target, and every output is checked
  exactly against (1 + ε)δ.

**A pair** is one trajectory, one baseline, one target f and one ε. Its **size ratio** is ours'
vertices over the baseline's, in percent. Under 100, ours is smaller, both within the same T.

## Where the data is

- `data/target-matched/greedy-stabbing-shared/eps<ε>/summary/<dataset>/<name>.json`: one per
  trajectory and ε, written by `ssk_target_match` (step 8). Each entry of `pairs` has
  `algorithm`, `level` (`f0.003`, …), `target` (T), `baseline.output_size`,
  `baseline.distance`, `baseline.input`, `ours.delta`, `ours.output_size` and `ours.within_target`.
- Ours' curves are under `data/target-matched/greedy-stabbing-shared/eps<ε>/results/`. The
  baselines' curves and searches are under `data/target-simplified/`, from `ssk_target_search`.
- The tables below are written by `scripts/ratio_percentiles.py`, with the ratios of
  `scripts/ratio_buckets.py` (step 12).

## How to read the tables

There is one table per ε and target f, with a row per baseline: the percentiles of the size
ratio over that row's pairs, pooled over the nine datasets. A percentile is the nearest rank:
pK is the smallest ratio with at least K% of the pairs at or below it. **worst** is the largest
ratio, the single pair where ours did worst against that baseline. Lower is better for ours in
every column.

## The goal

This experiment answers the question of a user with an error budget: **given T, who keeps fewer
vertices?** Experiment 1 matches ours to an error the baseline happened to reach. Here both
sides get the same budget, and the baselines are no longer held to a size they were not
designed for. If experiments 1 and 2 agree, neither result comes from how its experiment was
set up.

## Caveats

- **The baselines' searches find a boundary, not a minimum.** None of the four is monotone in
  its parameter: a kept vertex forces the matching through it, so fewer vertices can be closer.
  The setting found is within T and the next smaller one is not, so its size is the fewest
  found, not the fewest possible.
- **Where no setting is within T, the baseline is counted with the whole input.**
  - This happens in 25 860 of SQUISH-E's pairs per ε (10.9%), 27 996 of SQUISH's (11.8%) and
    2 750 of DPn's (1.2%), and most of them at f = 0.0003.
  - These pairs say more about the baseline's parameters than about ours. On a trace that no
    one can simplify within T, ours keeps nearly every point too, so such pairs sit near 100%.
    That is why the median against SQUISH and SQUISH-E is higher at f = 0.0003 than at
    f = 0.001 (44% against 30% for SQUISH at ε = 0.1).
- **Coverage gaps:**
  - DOTS' search does not finish in minutes on the 31 trajectories of more than 30 000
    vertices and on one Mopsi trajectory, so DOTS has 32 fewer pairs at each target.
  - 365 trajectories have no extent: every point is the same, and every target would be 0, so
    they have no pairs.
- **Ours places new points**, while every baseline keeps a subsequence of the input.
- **No baseline minimises the Fréchet distance.** DPn works with the distance to a line, the
  other three with the synchronous Euclidean distance.
- **The search costs DOTS its online property**, as it does in every experiment.
- **A fraction of the diagonal is a different tolerance on different shapes.** It is loose on
  a long straight highway track, and tight on a pedestrian trace that doubles back.
- **Pooled over nine datasets.** The per-dataset tables are in `data/ratio-buckets.json`.
